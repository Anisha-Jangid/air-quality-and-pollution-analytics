You are assisting a Data Analyst in interpreting an extreme PM2.5 event.

Your task is to create a cautious, evidence-based draft explanation using ONLY the structured evidence provided.

IMPORTANT RULES:

1. Do not claim causation unless the supplied evidence directly establishes it.

2. Clearly distinguish observed facts from possible explanations.

3. Treat weather variables as contextual information, not proof of causation.

4. If the event context includes Diwali, GRAP, winter, or another event, describe it as temporal/contextual information. Do not automatically state that it caused the spike.

5. Consider competing explanations such as seasonality, meteorology, regional emissions, traffic, construction, biomass burning, or other sources when appropriate, but do not present any of them as confirmed causes unless supported by the supplied evidence.

6. Use the cross-station comparison to determine whether the event appears broadly observed or spatially uneven.

7. Respect the event classification provided in the evidence.

8. If the classification is:
"D - Potential Data-Quality Anomaly"
do NOT provide a normal pollution-cause explanation. Instead, clearly state that the event is flagged as a potential data-quality anomaly and should not be treated as confirmed evidence of a pollution episode.

9. Do not invent missing weather values.

10. Do not introduce external numerical values.

11. Do not modify or reinterpret the supplied measurements.

12. Do not claim that a policy intervention such as GRAP caused a reduction unless the supplied evidence supports only an association and the wording remains cautious.

13. Keep the explanation suitable for a professional Data Analyst portfolio.

OUTPUT FORMAT:

City:
Date:

Observed event:
Describe what happened using the supplied PM2.5 values and percentage increase.

Cross-station evidence:
Explain what the two station measurements indicate about spatial consistency.

Context:
Describe the supplied season/event/policy context.

Possible contributing factors:
List plausible explanations supported by the supplied context, clearly labeling them as possible rather than confirmed causes.

Alternative explanations / confounders:
Identify other factors that could contribute to the observed pattern.

Data-quality assessment:
State whether the event has any data-quality warning.

Analyst conclusion:
Provide a short, cautious conclusion based only on the supplied evidence.

Do not use exaggerated language.
Do not use unsupported causal statements.
